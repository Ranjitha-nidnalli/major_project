Sugarcane farmers in Karnataka need timely answers on varieties, planting, fertiliser,
pests and diseases, in Kannada. Large language models (LLMs) can answer such questions
fluently, but in agriculture a fluent wrong answer is dangerous: a wrong pesticide or a
wrong dose can destroy a crop or harm the person spraying it. This project builds
\emph{Krishi Mitra}, a Kannada question-answering assistant for sugarcane, and treats
\emph{refusing} as always better than \emph{guessing}.

We built and evaluated two versions. Version~1 is a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
pipeline: BGE-M3 dense and learned-sparse embeddings, hybrid retrieval with reciprocal
rank fusion in Qdrant, an abstention gate, LLM generation in Kannada, and two answer
checks (an LLM faithfulness judge and a rule-based numeric checker). While building it we
found and fixed several failures that silently made the system less safe, including a
confidence gate that read a rank-based score (an unanswerable question scored 1.0) and
router and judge calls that failed silently because a reasoning model used up its token
budget. We also show that ROUGE-L does not work on Kannada text (6 of 16 reference answers
score 0.000 against themselves). On 40 held-out questions, version~1 made the correct
answer-or-refuse decision on 29: 14 of 16 questions that should be refused were refused and
15 of 24 answerable questions were answered. One answer gave the dose for the wrong pest,
and both automatic checks rated it as fully faithful.

Version~2 removes the LLM from the answer path. Questions are matched deterministically to
typed fact records drafted from the University of Agricultural Sciences (UAS) Bengaluru
Package of Practices 2025 and UAS Dharwad Package of Practices 2020, each with a verbatim
source excerpt and printed page number. The assistant answers only from records that a
person has confirmed against the printed page, and refuses everything else with the Kisan
