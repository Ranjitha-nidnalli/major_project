\chapter{Literature Survey}
\label{ch:literature}

\section{Survey method}
Papers were found by searching arXiv, the ACL Anthology and Google Scholar for
agricultural chatbots and question answering (including systems built on Kisan Call Centre
data), retrieval-augmented generation, multilingual embeddings, Indic-language benchmarks,
hallucination and abstention in LLMs, and text-generation metrics. We selected 25 works that
bear directly on the design or evaluation of this project. Their summaries below, including
all reported numbers, are taken from each paper's abstract; the numbers are the authors'
own and were measured on their own data, so they are not comparable with each other or with
ours.

\section{Agricultural question answering and advisory systems}

\paragraph{Krishi Sathi~\cite{vijayvargia2025krishisathi}.}
A multi-turn agricultural chatbot for Indian farmers. Instead of answering one-off
questions, it follows an intent-driven dialogue to collect the details it needs, then
retrieves from a curated agricultural database (RAG) and generates the answer with an
instruction-fine-tuned model trained on three Indian agricultural datasets. It supports
English and Hindi with speech. Reported: 97.53\% query response accuracy, 91.35\%
contextual relevance and personalisation, 97.53\% query completion, and under 6 seconds
average response time. \emph{Limitation for our purpose:} the accuracy figures do not show
how often the system declines questions it cannot answer, and Kannada is not covered.

\paragraph{Farmer.Chat~\cite{singh2024farmerchat}.}
A generative-AI agricultural assistant from Digital Green, using RAG over unstructured
sources such as documents, crop tables and videos, built to move past the deterministic
dialogue flows and limited language support of earlier tools. It has been deployed in four
countries, engaged over 15,000 farmers and answered over 300,000 queries. \emph{Relevance:}
shows that farmers use such assistants at scale, which makes the safety of each answer
matter more.

\paragraph{AgroLLM~\cite{samuel2025agrollm}.}
A RAG chatbot using a FAISS vector database over an open-source agricultural knowledge base,
comparing Gemini 1.5 Flash, ChatGPT-4o Mini and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 across four
agricultural domains. ChatGPT-4o Mini with RAG reached the highest accuracy, 93\%.
\emph{Limitation:} accuracy on answerable questions only; English.

\paragraph{RAG advisory framework~\cite{sawant2026empowering}.}
A RAG system over Package of Practices documents for five crops (maize, ragi, sweet potato,
cotton, groundnut), with semantic chunking, Amazon Titan embeddings and ChromaDB, comparing
Llama~3.1, Mistral, Phi-3 and Qwen~2.5. Retrieval was measured with precision@K, recall@K,
MRR and nDCG; Mistral and Qwen~2.5 performed best. \emph{Relevance:} closest to our source
choice (university Packages of Practices), but answers are still generated by an LLM.

\paragraph{AgriLLM~\cite{didwania2024agrillm}.}
Applies transformer LLMs to automate answers to farmer queries, using about 4 million
helpline queries from Tamil Nadu across sectors, seasonal crops and query types. The
abstract reports no quantitative results. \emph{Relevance:} shows the scale of real farmer
questions available from the Kisan Call Centre.

\paragraph{KisanQRS~\cite{rehman2024kisanqrs}.}
A query-response system built on 34 million Kisan Call Centre call logs. It maps a new query
to past queries using semantic and lexical similarity with fast threshold-based clustering
(LSTM chosen for query mapping), and retrieves answers by clustering candidate answers and
choosing cluster leaders. Evaluated on 300,000 samples from five states: top F1 of 96.58\%
for query mapping in one state, and NDCG of 96.20\% for answer retrieval (10,000 samples).
\emph{Limitation:} reuses past call-centre answers, which may be outdated or region-specific.

\paragraph{Agribot~\cite{jain2025agribot}.}
A chatbot on the Kisan Call Centre dataset covering weather, market rates, plant protection
and government schemes, using a sentence embedding model. Accuracy rose from 56\% to 86\%
after removing synonyms and adding entity extraction. \emph{Relevance:} entity handling made
the largest difference, which matches our finding that a wrong-entity match is the most
dangerous retrieval error.

\paragraph{AgAsk~\cite{koopman2022agask}.}
A conversational agent that answers farmers' questions from scientific documents. The
authors release a test collection of real farmer questions, scientific documents split into
passages, and relevance judgements, and show that two neural ranking models match passages to
questions effectively. It is deployed through Telegram on commodity hardware.
\emph{Relevance:} returns passages rather than generated text, a design closer to ours.

\paragraph{GPT-4 as an agronomist assistant~\cite{silva2023gpt4agronomist}.}
Evaluates Llama~2 and GPT models, with RAG and ensemble refinement, on agriculture exams and
benchmarks from Brazil, India and the USA. GPT-4 reached 93\% accuracy on certification-exam
questions, against 88\% for earlier models. \emph{Limitation:} exam accuracy is not the same
as safe field advice; a model can pass an exam and still give a wrong dose.

\paragraph{GPT-4 as evaluator for pest management~\cite{yang2024gpt4evaluator}.}
Uses GPT-4 to score pest-management advice from GPT and FLAN models on coherence, logical
consistency, fluency, relevance, comprehensibility and exhaustiveness, with an expert system
built on crop threshold data as a factual baseline. With domain-specific instruction
prompting, accuracy was 72\%. \emph{Relevance:} LLM judges score qualities such as fluency
and relevance; our results show that such a judge can rate a wrong-pest answer as fully
faithful.

\section{Indic-language question answering and evaluation}

\paragraph{Indic QA Benchmark~\cite{singh2024indicqa}.}
A context-grounded QA dataset for 11 major Indian languages with extractive and abstractive
tasks. Multilingual LLMs performed poorly in low-resource Indian languages because of English
bias in training data, and translating to English first (``translate-test'') did better,
especially in low-resource settings.

\paragraph{L3Cube-IndicQuest~\cite{rohera2024indicquest}.}
A gold-standard factual QA benchmark of 200 question-answer pairs in each of English and 19
Indic languages, including Kannada, across five India-specific domains, supporting
reference-based and LLM-as-judge evaluation.

\paragraph{IndicGenBench~\cite{singh2024indicgenbench}.}
A generation benchmark for 29 Indic languages (13 scripts, 4 language families) covering
cross-lingual summarisation, translation and cross-lingual QA. Among GPT-3.5, GPT-4, PaLM-2,
mT5, Gemma, BLOOM and LLaMA, the largest PaLM-2 did best on most tasks, but every model showed
a significant gap compared with English.

\paragraph{IndicMT Eval~\cite{sai2023indicmteval}.}
7,000 fine-grained human (MQM) annotations of translations into 5 Indian languages from 7
systems, used to test 16 metrics. Pre-trained metrics such as COMET correlated best with
human judgement, n-gram metrics such as BLEU worse, and no metric captured fluency errors in
Indian languages well. \emph{Relevance:} independent support for our finding that surface
metrics are unreliable for Kannada.

\section{Retrieval-augmented generation and retrieval}

\paragraph{RAG~\cite{lewis2020rag}.}
Introduced retrieval-augmented generation: a pre-trained sequence-to-sequence model
(parametric memory) combined with a dense vector index of Wikipedia (non-parametric memory)
accessed by a neural retriever. It set the state of the art on three open-domain QA tasks and
generated more specific, diverse and factual text than a parametric-only baseline.

\paragraph{BGE-M3~\cite{chen2024bgem3}.}
An embedding model supporting retrieval in more than 100 languages, performing dense,
multi-vector and sparse retrieval from one model, for inputs up to 8,192 tokens. It is trained
with self-knowledge distillation, using the combined relevance scores of the different
retrieval modes as the teacher signal, and reports state-of-the-art multilingual,
cross-lingual and long-document retrieval. We use it for both the dense and the learned
sparse vectors.

\paragraph{Reciprocal rank fusion~\cite{cormack2009rrf}.}
A simple way to combine ranked lists, scoring each document by $\sum 1/(k+r)$ over the lists
it appears in. On TREC experiments it beat every individual system and Condorcet fusion.
Because the score depends only on ranks, it says nothing about absolute relevance, which we
found matters for abstention (Section~\ref{sec:defects}).

\paragraph{BM25~\cite{robertson2009bm25}.}
The probabilistic relevance framework behind BM25 and BM25F, the standard lexical ranking
functions. We use BM25 fused with dense retrieval for pest and disease questions, where exact
names matter.

\paragraph{RAGAS~\cite{es2023ragas}.}
A framework for evaluating RAG pipelines without human reference answers, using LLM-based
metrics for retrieval relevance, faithfulness of the answer to the retrieved passages, and
answer quality. \emph{Limitation shown by our results:} faithfulness to the retrieved passage
cannot detect that the passage is about the wrong pest.

\section{Hallucination and abstention}

\paragraph{Hallucination survey~\cite{huang2024hallucination}.}
A taxonomy of LLM hallucination (plausible but non-factual content), its causes, detection
methods and benchmarks, and mitigation strategies, including the current limits of
retrieval-augmented LLMs.

\paragraph{Abstention survey~\cite{wen2025abstention}.}
Surveys abstention, the refusal of an LLM to answer, as a way to reduce hallucination and
improve safety, analysed from the perspectives of the query, the model and human values, and
reviews abstention methods, benchmarks and metrics. It asks whether abstention can become a
general capability across tasks and domains.

\paragraph{Selective question answering~\cite{kamath2020selective}.}
Tests QA models on a mix of in-domain and out-of-domain questions, where the model must answer
as many as possible while keeping accuracy high. Abstaining on the model's own softmax
probability works poorly because models are over-confident out of domain; a trained
calibrator that predicts errors answers 56\% of questions at 80\% accuracy, against 48\%
using the probabilities. \emph{Relevance:} model confidence is not a safe signal for refusing,
which is why version~2 decides by whether a confirmed record exists, not by a score.

\section{Text-generation metrics}

\paragraph{BLEU~\cite{papineni2002bleu}, ROUGE~\cite{lin2004rouge} and chrF~\cite{popovic2015chrf}.}
BLEU scores word n-gram precision against reference translations; ROUGE (including ROUGE-L,
based on the longest common subsequence) was designed for summaries; chrF uses character
n-gram F-scores, which its author proposed as less dependent on tokenisation and better
suited to morphologically rich languages. All three measure overlap with a reference text.
Our experiments (Section~\ref{sec:metric-failures}) show that ROUGE-L fails on Kannada, and
that all three reward a dose error that keeps the reference wording.

\section{Research gap}
The agricultural systems surveyed report accuracy, relevance or user reach on questions they
are expected to answer. None of the abstracts reports how often a system correctly refuses a
question it should not answer, or counts answers that give a wrong dose. Most generate the
answer text with an LLM, and those that use LLM judges evaluate fluency, relevance or
faithfulness to the retrieved passage, which does not detect an answer about the wrong pest.
Indic benchmarks show that LLMs are weaker in low-resource languages, and metric studies show
that n-gram metrics are unreliable for Indian languages. Within the papers we found, Kannada
agricultural advice and its safety evaluation are not covered. This project addresses that
gap: a Kannada assistant whose evaluation counts correct refusals and wrong doses, and whose
answer path gives doses only from person-confirmed records of official recommendations.
